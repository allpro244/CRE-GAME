## Summary

**Counts by bucket** (table rows; one row can cover several related lines): FAKE 89 (high 9, med 33, low 47; this includes rows marked "partial" or "minor"), EXOGENOUS 34, GUARD 15, PHYSICAL 13.

**What happens most often.** In almost every negotiation in these files, the model already computes a reservation price, then decides the outcome with a die that ignores it.
- Owners have a computed `number` / reserve (actions.ts:2140), but the counter and bid outcomes are slopes plus `rng` (actions.ts:2359, 2416-2470, 2285).
- Tenants have a computed indifference point `fStar` (leasing.ts:3369), but acceptance is a logit roll that runs again on every counter (4441 / 5129).
- Sellers on the tape have an asserted reserve of 0.92 × ask.

The general fix is the same each time: draw the private value once, store it, and make the outcome deterministic against it.

**The top 5 fakes to fix first**

1. **Street deal rent noise. streetRolls.ts:619, `rrange(0.94,1.03)`.** Every street lease's rent is a die with mean 0.985. It goes through `recordDeal` into `dealSlack`, and from there into the market rent index, every month. So the city's rent level has a built-in downward drift of about 1.5% per deal, and it has no cause. Fix it with a Nash bargain between the firm's reservation rent (from its books and its next-best suite in the same search loop) and the landlord's (market × staleDiscount × carry cost of the vacancy).
2. **Free rent and TI drawn uniformly, on the street and for the player.** The lines are streetRolls.ts:631 (`·rng()`), leasing.ts:2356 (`rrange(0.25,0.85)`), and leasing.ts:2346, 1931 and 2060 (TI anywhere in the band). Concessions are the main lever on effective rent, and here they are dice. Fix: one net-effective identity, so the face rent comes from the bargain, free months clear NE to `effRentIdx`, and TI is the fit-out cost of the suite (shell share or fit age × cost × `costIdx`).
3. **The player tenant's opening bid. leasing.ts:2275**: 30% chance of 0.68-0.86, otherwise 0.9-1.1. It sits alongside rentBias at 2262. The comment says the spread is there to make the modal interesting, which makes it a game-design constant wearing an economic label. The bid should be the seeker firm's willingness to pay (`entrantMargin`) against its alternatives in `e.seekers`, which `pullSeeker` already draws from.
4. **The renewal decision and the renewal rent. leasing.ts:1171-1237 and 1999-2001.** By the code's own comment, `renewalIntent` sits on its 0.96 ceiling at neutral settings, so the rail is load-bearing. Its input is a product of ten asserted step factors. The renewal rent is another ladder (0.82/0.94/1.0, with clamps 0.85-1 and 0.78-1.18, floored at 0.6×market). The street has a separate, partly sourced expiry model (streetRolls.ts:331-355), so the same question has two answers. Fix: put player tenants through the street's expiry logic (stay versus the best alternative in the pool, net of moving cost), and set renewal rent as a split of the moving-cost surplus. The inputs already exist in `renewalReserve` (leasing.ts:5405+).
5. **City tape liquidity and pricing. actions.ts:3851-3852, 3868, 3895, 3907, 3917.** Whether a listing trades this month is an asserted hazard: 0.02+0.08·hot…, priceFactor clamped to [0.3,1.8], a floor of 0.25 on appetite. Then `max(1, qualifiedBuyers)` invents a phantom bidder when nobody qualifies, and the reserve is 0.92×ask by assertion. This sets city transaction volume and comps, which feed the cap-rate index. The second-price auction at 3905-3922 is already the right mechanism. Run it on every listing every month, with real qualified firms plus a calibrated exogenous outside-buyer arrival rate, and drop the arrival roll and the phantom bidder. The player's sale paths (2645-2710, 3832-3834) and auction.ts:245-251 should then use the same auction.

The next fixes after these: the owner and tenant accept rolls against an already-known reserve (actions.ts:2359 and 2416-2470, leasing.ts:4441 and 5129); NPL offers created on loans that are actually current (notes.ts:603-656); note maturity and default dice instead of the refinancing and arrears tests rivalDebt already supports (notes.ts:225, 240); and two answers to one quantity, player versus street, for tenant headcount (leasing.ts:1835) and flat turnover (leasing.ts:1409-1411).

**Fixes that look risky**

- **#1 and #2, street rent and concessions.** These feed `recordDeal` → `dealSlack` → the rent index, which is a closed loop. Removing the 1.5% downward bias, or replacing the dice with a bargain, will shift city rent levels and could expose upstream compensations. The entry rail (streetRolls.ts:516, cap 1.6) and the comment about rents tripling suggest earlier tuning rested on this noise. Expect BASELINE.json to move a lot. Land these separately, with `pnpm baseline:check` and a rent-chart run.
- **#4, renewal.** Per the code's own comments, the 0.96 ceiling and the factor product hold player occupancy where it is. The street model turns over more (about 55/65/75% retention), so player buildings' occupancy and value will probably fall. This needs a measured before/after on player occupancy.
- **#5, tape.** It is coupled to the cap-rate index through `recordComp`. Removing the phantom bidder in a credit crunch means real no-trade months. That is honest, but the cap-rate index will then update on thinner data, so check that it does not stall or jump.
- **Sale bids (actions.ts:2710).** The comments show this band was re-tuned at least twice to close a sell-high exploit (from a $73M to a $619M median). Switching to `firmMaxPrice` could reopen the exploit if rivals' max prices run above appraisal. Run the stress/exploit harness.
- **Accept rolls drawn once per LOI or listing.** They have to use the existing streams carefully. The comments warn that adding or removing draws re-rolls whole campaigns (owners.ts:774-789, leasing.ts:3862), so preserve the draw count or accept a baseline reset.

Not in my files, but worth a look: `LOI_ODDS_MAX` in absorption.ts:599 caps LOI arrival; `distressPrice` floor 0.62 in sponsor.ts:76; the `marketAppetite` and `qualifiedBuyers` clamps on creditIdx [0.4, 1.25] in rivals.ts:2432 and 5064.
